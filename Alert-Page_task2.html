Practical steps to reduce heat risk

Before going out

  • Plan strenuous work for cooler hours.
  • Carry drinking water and take regular breaks in shade.
  • Wear light, loose clothing and protect yourself from direct sun.

At home or work

  • Keep rooms cool with shade and airflow where safe.
  • Check on older adults, infants, and people who work outdoors.
  • Never leave children or anyone else in a parked vehicle.

Stay hydrated

  • Drink water regularly; do not wait until you feel very thirsty.
  • Use oral rehydration solution when appropriate and follow its instructions.
  • Limit alcohol and excessive caffeine during extreme heat.

Recognize a possible heat emergency

Confusion, fainting, seizures, very hot skin, or loss of consciousness can signal a medical emergency. Move the person to a cooler place, begin cooling them, and contact local emergency medical services immediately. Do not give fluids to someone who is unconscious or unable to swallow safely.
